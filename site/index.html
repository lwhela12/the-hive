Architects of Collective Abundance

Where brilliant minds don’t just network—they build.

Who we are

We’re a bunch of like-minded people who like looking after each other and our community, and we get together once a month. Between us we have almost everything anyone needs — it just sits scattered across a lot of heads. HIVE pools it.

What HIVE stands for

Human

Inspired by Isaac Asimov’s Trap Door Spider Society, Natalie Walstead took the idea and modernised it for a faster world.

Insight

A hurdle for you might be someone else’s hobby. An insurmountable mountain for you might be somebody’s Tuesday.

Vision

The first HIVE is local to Las Vegas, but the vision is wider than that, and open to anyone. We’re stronger together.

Execution

We meet monthly to brainstorm and then actually do it. Join in person or virtually, and catch up in the app.

Every month

Three parts to a month

  • HIVE MeetingOnce a month we work on each other’s HDs: high-definition wishes, sharpened in a Humdinger session until they’re specific enough for somebody to grant. Come in person, join on Google Meet, or catch up in your own time — busy lives are welcome here.
  • HIVE HelpWe get a lot out of giving back, so every month we pick a focus. Cleaning up a parking lot, grabbing trash on your walk, paying it behind at the drive-through, a donation to a shelter, fifteen minutes of kindness — every act counts, however small. Want the monthly spotlight on you, your project, or your business? Ask us.
  • HIVE HangYou can’t pour from an empty cup, so at least once a month we get together purely to enjoy each other. Sometimes it’s our own small crew, and sometimes it’s a more-is-more night out — a show, a concert, whatever sounds good.

    Checking what we’re helping with…

    HIVE is inspired by Isaac Asimov’s Trap Door Spider Society, adapted for a faster world. Here’s where that comes from.

    Once a month, a group of friends met for dinner. Every member brought a guest — and the whole evening was spent drawing that one person out.

    Asimov spent years at a monthly dinner club in New York. In his autobiography I, Asimov he wrote about it as the Trap Door Spider Society, and turned it into fiction as the Black Widowers — stories where a guest turns up with a problem he can’t solve, gets thoroughly questioned over dinner, and leaves with an answer none of them had on their own.

    Natalie Walstead took that idea and built on it: the same structure, a faster world, tools that keep it going between meetings, and a table that’s open to everyone.

    Mugs, journals, canvas totes, shirts — we put the bee on all of it and made a batch for everyone. It doesn’t fund anything or mean anything. It’s just cool. More designs on the way.
